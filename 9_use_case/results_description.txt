DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF RESULTS & MODEL-SPECIFIC CUTOFF ADOPTION
===================================================================

1. ADOPTION OF MODEL-SPECIFIC DECISION CUTOFFS
----------------------------------------------
Each model architecture (Traditional Baseline XGBoost vs. GraphAware XGBoost) possesses a unique probability output distribution. Using a single hardcoded cutoff across different architectures creates unfair bias.

We computed the optimal Geometric Mean (G-Mean) ROC decision cutoff thresholds on the validation set (MIMIC_CBC_BMP):
  - Traditional Baseline XGBoost Cutoff:   0.001613
  - GraphAware XGBoost Cutoff:             0.000178

3. WHY TRADITIONAL XGBOOST FAILED AT SEPSIS ONSET
-------------------------------------------------
At t = 76.4h (Sepsis Onset), the patient's WBC count dropped to 11.1 k/uL (nearing reference range <= 11.0 k/uL). Because Traditional XGBoost evaluates each blood draw in isolation based purely on static tabular values, seeing a near-normal WBC caused Traditional XGBoost's estimated risk to plummet to 0.000599 (well below its model-specific cutoff 0.001613).
Traditional XGBoost was fooled by the pseudo-normalization of WBC, creating a dangerous False Negative.


4. WHY GRAPHAWARE (XGBOOST) SUCCEEDED
-------------------------------------
GraphAware evaluates user-defined temporal neighborhood feature aggregations (`original_features - mean_neighbors`).
Instead of looking at WBC = 11.1 k/uL in isolation, GraphAware incorporates the dynamic delta between current values and prior patient state trajectory. GraphAware recognized that despite the static WBC value appearing normal, the underlying temporal trajectory across blood draws indicated evolving systemic infection.
GraphAware predicted 0.003936 (well above its cutoff 0.000178 -> TRUE POSITIVE), successfully detecting sepsis at onset with >6.5x higher probability than Traditional XGBoost.
